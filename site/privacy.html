Privacy, offline use and terms

Your data stays on this device

  • R/D runs entirely in your browser. Drawings, notes and settings are saved only in this browser on this device. Nothing you draw or type is uploaded, and there are no accounts, analytics, trackers or cookies.
  • Dictation uses your browser’s speech recognition. Where the browser can recognise speech on the device (recent Chrome on a computer), R/D asks it to, so the audio stays here. Otherwise the browser may send the audio to its own speech service (Google for Chrome, Apple for Safari) to be transcribed — so don’t dictate patient names or other identifiers.
  • The site’s host sees ordinary page requests, as for any website. The fonts and icons are served from this site, not from third parties.
  • The Send feedback button opens a Google Form in a new tab. Only what you type into that form is sent, to Google and to the R/D author under Google’s privacy policy, and only when you submit it. Your drawing is never attached.
  • Teaching-review links carry the drawing, comments and names inside the link itself, after the “#”, which browsers do not send to the web server. Anyone who has the link can open it, so share it only through channels your institution allows, and keep patient identifiers out of labels and comments.

Use it like an app

  • After the first visit R/D works offline. On iPhone or iPad: Share → Add to Home Screen. On Android or in Chrome: the menu → Install app.
  • Clearing this browser’s site data deletes your saved drawing; save a PNG of anything you want to keep.

Intended use

  • R/D is a drawing, documentation and education aid for eye-care professionals and trainees. It is not medical advice. It does not diagnose, treat or replace an examination, and it is not intended for patients or caregivers making decisions about their own care.
  • The repair-options section summarises published studies for education. It is not a recommendation for any individual patient, it is not designed for time-critical decisions, and it should not be relied on in place of the clinician’s own review of the sources and the patient.
